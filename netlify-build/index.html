How you get paid

Cost per lead vs pay per call

The network buys on two different models, and mixing them up will wreck your maths.

$

Cost per lead (CPL)

Median payouts $33 – $210

You’re paid when a submitted lead converts to a booked appointment. Payouts are much higher — restoration niches clear $180–$500 — because a lot of raw leads never make it that far.

☏

Pay per call

Median payouts $5 – $43

You’re paid for a qualified inbound phone call that stays connected past a minimum duration. Payouts are lower per event, but a far larger share of your traffic qualifies.

A $210 biohazard CPL payout and a $32.50 gutters call payout are not comparable numbers. Judge each against the traffic cost and conversion rate of its own model, not against each other.

Geography

Why payouts vary between ZIP codes

Buyers bid per ZIP based on how badly contractors in that area want work, so the same niche can pay several times more one county over. Electrical spans $1.04 to $74.03 — a 71× spread. HVAC, Plumbing and Roofing behave the same way.

But not every niche is priced geographically. Gutters pays one flat rate in all 36,000+ of its ZIP codes. In these niches there is no high-payout ZIP to hunt for and ranking cities by payout is meaningless — your only lever is search volume and cost per click.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How much do home-service leads pay?
It depends heavily on the niche. In this network, restoration work pays most — biohazard cleanup, fire damage and water damage all sit near $190–$210 at the median and reach $500 in the strongest ZIP codes. Remodeling niches such as kitchens, siding and windows run roughly $100–$120. Trade call niches such as plumbing, HVAC and roofing typically pay $30–$45 per qualified call, and appliance repair is the cheapest at around $5.50.
Which lead generation niche pays the most?
By median payout, biohazard cleanup is the highest at about $210 per lead, followed by fire damage removal and water damage restoration. Water damage has the single highest payout of any ZIP code in the network at $507. These are all cost-per-lead niches, so payout is only earned on a booked appointment.
How do I check whether my city or ZIP code is covered?
Use the “By city” search at the top of this page. Enter a city name or a five-digit ZIP code and you’ll see every niche bought in that market with its payouts and ZIP counts. If a city isn’t covered, the tool automatically lists covered cities within 40 miles.
What’s the difference between CPL and pay per call?
Cost per lead pays when a submitted lead becomes a booked appointment, which is why those payouts are much larger. Pay per call pays for a qualified inbound call that lasts past a minimum duration, so payouts are smaller but a much larger share of traffic qualifies. The two models can’t be compared on payout alone.
How often is the data updated?
Coverage and payouts are pulled from LeadSmart’s live feed once every day and the site is rebuilt automatically. The exact date of the current dataset is shown at the bottom of this page.
Does a high payout mean it’s a good market?
No. Payout is only the revenue side. A market is worth entering when payout times conversion rate exceeds your cost to acquire the traffic, which depends on search volume, cost per click and how strong the existing local competition is. Several niches here also pay a flat rate nationwide, so ranking their cities by payout tells you nothing at all.

Where the data comes from. Coverage and payout figures come from LeadSmart’s public affiliate bid feed and are resynchronised daily. City, county, population and density are matched from the SimpleMaps US Cities dataset (CC BY 4.0); roughly 13% of ZIP codes in the feed are administrative or PO-box ZIPs with no matching city record, and those show no population figure.
